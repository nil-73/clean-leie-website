What's that green stuff in the canal?

Three things cause most of the green water in Ghent. Here's how to tell them apart and which one to watch out for.

Blue-green algae can be toxic. If the water looks like spilled green paint or has a scum layer, keep people and dogs out of it and don't touch it. Check this advice against the official VMM / Agentschap Zorg en Gezondheid guidance on blue-green algae.

Duckweed (kroos)

Not harmful
How to recognise it
A bright green carpet on the surface made of tiny round leaves, each a few millimetres wide. It drifts with the wind and piles up in corners and along docks.
What it is
A tiny floating plant, not an algae.
Problem for businesses
Gets sucked into boat cooling intakes and propellers, and looks unappealing next to terraces.
Why it grows
Lots of nutrients in the water and calm, sheltered spots where it isn't washed away.

Filamentous algae

Nuisance, harm level
How to recognise it
Long, slimy green threads, sometimes floating as mats. Often attached to docks, canal walls and boat hulls.
Problem for businesses
Wraps around propellers and clogs filters. Rotting mats can smell in hot weather.
Why it grows
Nutrients (nitrates and phosphates), sunlight and warm water.

Blue-green algae (blauwalg)

Can be toxic
How to recognise it
Water that looks like pea soup or spilled green or blue-green paint, sometimes with a scum layer that collects along the edges. Often smells bad.
What it is
Technically not algae but bacteria (cyanobacteria).
Is it harmful?
Some blooms make toxins that can cause skin rashes and stomach problems in people, and can be deadly for dogs. Health effects and advice
Problem for businesses
Smell near terraces, customers avoiding the water, and possible warnings or bans on water activities.
Why it grows
Hot, sunny weather, still or slow water and lots of nutrients.

Where the nutrients come from

All three grow faster when the water holds a lot of nitrates and phosphates. In Ghent much of this comes from fertilizer that washes off farmland upstream, plus waste water from industry and households. Ghent's historic canals are slow and shallow, so the water warms up quickly in summer. Sources of nutrients in the Leie and Ghent canals
